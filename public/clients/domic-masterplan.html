The goal

Qualified conversations, not more traffic

Every hour an engineer spends quoting a project that was never viable is an hour that should have gone to one that was. The whole plan exists to make sure the people who reach a call have already told us they can afford the work.

The engine

Each piece feeds the next

This is not a list of separate tactics. It is a chain where each link exists because it makes the next one possible. If one fails, the ones after it cannot compensate.

01

Brand voice and positioning

The questionnaire captures how Domic speaks, what it refuses to say, and the philosophy behind the offer. From there comes the messaging for each audience.

Why it comes first: everything downstream is written in this voice. Producing content before this is settled means producing content that has to be redone.
02

The calculator does the filtering

The visitor shapes their own scope and watches cost move with their priorities. Simple enough not to overwhelm, and without exposing the internal reasoning behind the estimate.

What it replaces: the first qualifying call. By the time someone books, they have already seen a number and stayed. That is the filter working before anyone spends time on it.
03

Two paths, two messages

Homeowners get the experience of living in the house. Architects and developers get how straightforward Domic is to work with and the technical detail a project team needs.

Why they cannot share a page: a homeowner reading integration specs leaves, and an architect reading lifestyle copy does not trust it. Same brand, different proof.
04

Tracking before spend

CRM and analytics in place so every click, lead and conversion is attributable. Nothing goes live until this is running.

Why it is not optional: media without measurement is how agency budgets disappear without anyone being able to say what happened. This is the piece that makes the rest accountable.
05

Campaigns that qualify at the ad

Meta and Google for homeowners, LinkedIn for the professional side. Investment range asked upfront, so unqualified traffic filters itself out before it costs anyone time.

What it produces: fewer leads than a broad campaign would, and a far higher share of them worth a conversation. That trade is deliberate.
06

The engineering service closes the loop

The USD 7,000 design step. It is the point where the funnel stops being marketing and becomes revenue.

Why it is the real finish line: a booked call is a promise; a paid engineering service is proof the whole chain worked. It is also the number that tells us what a qualified call is actually worth, which is what makes scaling a decision instead of a guess.
Where this can break. The most fragile piece is the calculator. If it asks too much, people leave; if it asks too little, unqualified prospects reach a call anyway and the filter does nothing. Getting that balance right is worth more than any amount of additional media spend.

How we know it is working

Each piece has its own signal
PieceWorkingWarning sign
Messaging People arrive at a call already using Domic's language about their project. Every call starts from zero explaining what Domic does.
Calculator A healthy share of visitors finish it, and the ranges they land on are realistic. High starts and low completions: it is asking too much.
Campaigns Cost per qualified call trending down as we optimize. Cheap clicks that never reach the calculator.
Qualification Most booked calls are worth having. Volume of calls with nothing behind them, which is the current problem restated.
Revenue Engineering services sold at a predictable rate per call. Good calls that never convert: the gap is in the offer, not the marketing.
The number that makes scaling a decision. Once we know what a qualified call costs and how many turn into engineering services, we know what we can afford to pay for the next one. Until then, every dollar of media is buying information rather than return, and we will report it that way.
