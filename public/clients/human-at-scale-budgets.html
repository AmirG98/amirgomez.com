Reading these numbers. Both channels buy attention efficiently. A 3.4% click-through rate is strong for LinkedIn, and $0.43 per landing page view is low for a B2B accounting audience. They are doing different jobs: LinkedIn buys engagement on Chase's own content, Meta buys traffic to the Operations Assessment.
The gap, stated plainly. Neither number can be tied to a lead yet. Without the Meta pixel and the CRM connection, we see traffic arriving and lose it at the door. Until that is in place this page reports cost per click, not cost per lead, and no spend decision should be made on these figures alone. Installing the pixel is what turns this table into a real cost-per-lead view.
