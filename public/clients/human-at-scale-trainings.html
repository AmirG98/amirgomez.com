Anatomy of the winning post

The Germany post, dissected
1 · The human opening

Start with life, and a small confession

An honest, personal first line creates curiosity and lowers the guard. It reads like a person, not a brand.

"I\'ve been quiet because I\'ve been out of the office for the last three weeks."

2 · The pivot

Turn the life event into a business test

One sentence converts the trip into proof of the thesis. This is the move most founders miss: the story is not the point, it is the evidence.

"August was a huge month so far, and it was a complete test of what we\'ve built at Human at Scale."

3 · The honest middle

Admit the imperfection

"It doesn\'t mean there aren\'t hiccups" is what makes the claim believable. A flawless story reads as marketing; a true one reads as leadership.

4 · The teachable system

Give the numbered list

Four items, each one usable by the reader on their own firm. This is the authority payload: cadence, visibility, standards, accountability.

5 · The photo

Real, candid, faces visible

Not stock, not a graphic. A real table, real people, real trip. The photo is the proof that the story happened.

The four post archetypes

Rotate them; never repeat one twice in a row
Archetype A

Life as proof

A personal event becomes evidence of the professional thesis. The strongest format you have.

  • Trip, absence, family moment, health, sport
  • Pivot line: "and it tested everything we teach"
  • End with the system, numbered
Proven: the Germany post. 64+ reactions on a profile that averaged single digits on text posts.
Archetype B

Community scoreboard

Event recaps that tag people generously. Every tag borrows that person\'s network for a day.

  • Numbered list of moments, one person per line
  • Mix humor with genuine praise
  • Tags typed in the composer, picked from the dropdown (QA rule)
Proven: Bridging the Gap recap: 21 comments, and the event posts drove the month\'s peak (one hit 99 interactions).
Archetype C

Milestone with a give

Announce the win, then immediately hand the reader something: a teaser of the content, a code, an invite.

  • Excitement is allowed: "Woo, I get to speak at..."
  • Tease the actual insight, not just the event
  • Close with the giveaway and a clear CTA
Proven: Intuit Connect post: 1,309 impressions and 25 comments in a day, with the $300 code doing the closing.
Archetype D

Operator\'s notebook

The pure-authority post, kept alive with one human ingredient so it doesn\'t sink to the 3-reaction baseline.

  • One firm-owner scenario, told as a story with stakes
  • PAS-V structure: problem, agitation, solution, value
  • Attach a real photo or a behind-the-scenes shot whenever possible
Proven: the "Nine Big Moves" post worked because nearly every firm owner felt seen; failure post-mortems (hiring) hit 1,211 impressions.

The cadence

Supply rule: lifestyle posts cannot be manufactured on demand. Capture the raw material as it happens (photos at events, trips, sessions) and bank it. One good photo folder beats ten writing sessions.

Rules that protect the voice

The line between personal and unprofessional
  • Chase writes, we polish. Grammar, punctuation, line breaks and formatting only. Nothing gets rewritten; anything structural gets flagged for Chase to decide.
  • Lifestyle serves the thesis. Every personal story must land on the owner-bottleneck idea or a system the reader can use. If it can\'t pivot, it\'s an Instagram post, not a LinkedIn post.
  • Real photos only. Candid beats posed, faces beat landscapes, and no stock imagery ever.
  • Tags are typed, never pasted. A pasted @name publishes as plain text and burns the reach. This gate lives in the Publishing QA checklist.
  • Vulnerability with a floor. Hiccups, lessons and imperfections build trust; venting, clients\' identifiable problems and anything financial about Human at Scale stay out.
  • One idea per post. The numbered list can carry four items, but they all serve a single thesis.
