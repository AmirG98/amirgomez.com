The three layers

What each one is responsible for

Everything runs in one of three layers: demand creation teaches the market that the owner is the bottleneck, capture and qualification turns that attention into a named contact worth calling, and the operating layer keeps both repeatable instead of dependent on a good week.

Detail

Everything in this plan sits in one of three layers, and they run in order. The first creates demand, the second captures and filters it, and the third keeps both repeatable. Read them top to bottom and the rest of this document explains how each piece fits.

Layer one

Demand creation

Teaching the market that the owner is the bottleneck, and that it is an operations problem rather than a hiring one. This is slow, compounding work and it is the part that cannot be bought.

  • Ops Notes holds the cadence and owns the relationship with the list.
  • LinkedIn is where Chase is visible as a person, not a company. Constant presence is the point: the audience compounds and does not reset when a campaign pauses. The account has already tested the alternative and the three campaigns that tried to capture signups directly were pulled.
  • Webinars and podcasts borrow audiences that already trust someone else.
  • Case studies prove the idea works, which is why only accounting firms get published.
Layer two

Capture and qualification

Turning interest into a measurable action, and filtering early so time goes to firms that fit. The Operations Assessment is the hinge: it is useful enough to be worth completing and revealing enough to qualify.

  • Meta lead forms remove the friction of an external form and now capture at $4.41 per lead. They are being rebuilt to qualify as they capture, because without questions about the firm, low friction produces volume that does not fit.
  • The Assessment is where interest becomes a named contact with context attached.
  • Nurture by segment, so a disqualified firm gets the newsletter and nothing else.
  • The Gusto partnership supplies eight pre-qualified firms without any acquisition cost.
Layer three

The operating layer

What keeps the first two layers repeatable instead of dependent on any single good week. This is the part that is being built right now.

  • Approvals, transcripts and the idea bank keep work visible rather than scattered across chat.
  • A quality checklist for anything published, so tags render and links resolve before it goes out.
  • Frameworks for webinars and podcasts, so results come from the process rather than improvisation.
  • Tracking, which is the piece currently missing and the reason the other two layers cannot be judged fairly.

What we are aiming at

The one outcome everything serves

Qualified conversations with owners of US accounting firms that match the ICP. Not traffic, not followers, not registrations. A firm that is too small gets disqualified rather than nurtured.

Detail

Qualified conversations with owners of US accounting firms who match the ICP. Not traffic, not followers, not registrations. A firm that is too small gets disqualified rather than nurtured, because the Audit only pays off for firms with enough complexity to have a bottleneck worth removing.

The thesis underneath all of it. The owner is the bottleneck. That is the belief the market has to accept before the Audit makes sense to them, which is why the content teaches the idea long before it sells the service.

How the pieces feed each other

The loop, in order

Chase writes, publishing earns authority, authority opens doors to webinars and podcasts, those leave recordings, recordings become content again. Paid does not start the loop, it amplifies it.

Detail
01

Chase writes

Ops Notes every other Friday, plus LinkedIn posts. This is the source material for everything downstream.

02

Authority opens doors

Published thinking is what makes a webinar host or a podcast say yes. Nobody books an unknown operator.

03

Appearances produce recordings

Every webinar and podcast leaves an asset. That asset is the point, whether two people attend or two hundred.

04

Recordings become content

Clips for LinkedIn, an Ops Notes issue, a replay for the list. The loop closes and starts again with more reach.

Why this order matters. Paid does not start the loop, it amplifies it. Running ads against content that has not proven itself organically means paying to distribute something we do not yet know works. That is why paid follows what already works organically: we are buying reach for winners, not searching for them with budget. LinkedIn is the exception that proves the rule, because there the spend is not amplifying a single post, it is keeping a constant stream of the right people arriving at the profile.

What the conversations are for

The offers everything upstream is pointing at

Each offer answers a different readiness level, so the job of a conversation is to find which one fits rather than to sell the largest.

Detail

A qualified conversation is not the goal in itself. It is the entry point to a ladder, and each rung asks for more commitment than the one before it. Knowing which rung a firm is ready for is what makes the conversation useful rather than exploratory.

Free

Operations Assessment

Self-diagnostic. Dynamic, so the questions follow the answers. This is where anonymous interest becomes a named contact with context attached.

Free

Firm Bottleneck Diagnostic

Thirty minutes with Chase. A working session, not a pitch. Produces a read on the real problem and one practical next step.

Paid · entry point

Operations Audit

How work actually flows, where the money is actually made, and what the tech stack does for the team versus to it. Diagnoses the main bottleneck and produces a sequenced roadmap for the next 60 days. It is the first phase of humanOS Lite rather than a standalone product, which is what makes it a natural opening rather than a commitment. The eight Gusto-nominated firms come in here.

Paid · ongoing

humanOS and humanOS Lite

Where the roadmap gets implemented instead of handed over. Two ways to run it: humanOS fully embedded on weekly execution, where the work is done for the firm, or humanOS Lite in two-week advisory cycles, where the firm's own team executes with guidance.

Why this matters for the channels. Every channel upstream is pointing at a different rung. Meta traffic goes to the Assessment because it is the lowest-commitment ask. LinkedIn and the newsletter build the trust that makes the Diagnostic worth booking. Webinars and podcasts borrow an audience that already trusts the host. When a channel points at the wrong rung, it produces volume that cannot convert.
Where this stands. humanOS has its own page and covers both delivery modes. The Operations Audit does not have one yet: today it is described inside the humanOS page as the first phase of Lite, which works for someone already reading about humanOS but gives us nowhere to send a reader who is only ready for the Audit. That is the page being built next with Makai Web, with the copy upleveled from the Notion source rather than ported across.

What has to be true next

Sequenced, not parallel

Two things, and they run together: one, a full quarter of the plan executed holistically across a period already heavy with webinars. Two, lead sources beyond webinars and podcasts, with Meta owning a source outright and quality as the open problem.

Detail

The pixel is in and recording, so the measurement question is answered. What comes next is not another piece of tooling. Two things have to be true, and neither of them happens on its own.

Condition one

A full quarter of the plan run holistically

Not the channels taken one at a time, but the whole thing executed together and in order, across a quarter that already has a heavy run of webinars in it. That timing is the opportunity: each appearance produces a recording, the recording produces clips and an Ops Notes issue, and that content is what makes the next appearance easier to book. The loop only compounds if every piece is actually running while the others are.

  • The webinars on the calendar get the full framework applied to each one, not just the sessions themselves.
  • Content, newsletter and LinkedIn stay on cadence through the quarter rather than pausing when a campaign does.
  • Paid runs alongside, so the audience being built organically has somewhere to convert.

Why a quarter. Anything shorter measures noise. The channels feed each other with a lag, so a month of coordinated execution tells us almost nothing about whether the model works.

Condition two

Lead sources beyond webinars and podcasts

Borrowed audiences are the strongest channel today, and that is also the risk: both depend on someone else saying yes. Meta is the obvious way to own a source outright, and the open problem is quality rather than volume. Native lead forms already capture cheaply, and the path from that form through to the Operations Assessment is where a raw lead has to turn into a qualified one.

  • Native forms first, because removing the external form is what makes the cost per lead workable at all.
  • The Assessment does the filtering, turning a name into a firm with context attached, which is what a sales call needs.
  • Qualify at capture, with questions about the firm inside the form, so the filtering does not wait for a booked call.

The number that matters. Not cost per lead but cost per lead that fits. A cheap lead from a firm too small to have a bottleneck worth removing costs more than an expensive one that does, because the expensive part is the call, not the click.

These two run together rather than in sequence. The quarter of coordinated execution is what makes the borrowed-audience channels pay off while they are available, and the Meta work is what stops the whole pipeline from depending on them. LinkedIn holds steady through both, because the profile traffic it buys is the input to the authority every other channel borrows.

What we will not do. Scale spend before attribution exists, publish case studies outside the ICP to fill space, or chase registration counts as if they were the goal. Each of those buys a number that looks like progress and is not.
