Webinar framework

Built after the Collaboration Room session
Why this exists. The Collaboration Room webinar drew one attendee, and he was disqualified on size. The session itself was not the problem: nothing in the process was set up to fill the room or to capture value afterwards. This framework fixes both, and it assumes every webinar is a content asset first and a lead source second.
4 weeks out

Qualify the room before committing

Before agreeing to a date, we settle three things in writing with the host.

  • Who attends. Firm size, role, and whether they match the ICP. One qualified owner beats forty bookkeepers.
  • What we can say. Some communities have a strict no-vendor policy. If we cannot pitch, the value has to come from the recording and the follow-up.
  • What we get afterwards. The attendee list, the recording, or neither. This is asked as a permission, never as an expectation.
3 weeks out

Lock the promise and the title

The title states the outcome, not the topic. "Removing the Owner Bottleneck: How to Make Your Firm Run Without You" works because a firm owner reads it and recognizes their own problem.

We also decide the single action an attendee should take, and that decision drives the whole session.

2 weeks out

Promote on both lists

  • Invitation to the Ops Notes list, plus a reminder the week of.
  • The host's own community, coordinated with them directly so we do not duplicate messaging.
  • Two LinkedIn posts from Chase, framed as the problem the session solves.

Registrations are tagged on the way in, so the follow-up can be segmented by source later.

During

Make the call to action explicit

When there is a no-vendor policy, traction has to come straight from the attendees. Chase shares his contact details out loud and puts a clear next step on screen, twice: once mid-session and once at the end.

The rule we learned the hard way. If nobody says the next step out loud, there is no next step. A recording with no call to action inside it cannot be repurposed into one later.
Within 48 hours

Harvest the recording

This is where a low-attendance session still pays for itself. From every recording we pull:

  • Three to five short clips for LinkedIn, cut around the concepts Chase flags.
  • One Ops Notes issue, or a section of one.
  • A replay link for the newsletter, so people who could not attend still see it.
Week after

Follow up by segment, not in bulk

  • Attended and qualified: personal note from Chase.
  • Registered, did not attend: replay link plus the one takeaway.
  • Attended and out of ICP: added to Ops Notes, no sales follow-up. Same call the Collaboration Room attendee got.

Podcast strategy

Based on the shows you sent through

Guesting is the fastest way to borrow an audience that already trusts someone else. In accounting the shows are concentrated, the hosts are known, and the listeners are the exact people we want. Three of them are worth pursuing now.

Priority one

The Accounting Podcast Best fit

Blake Oliver and David Leary. Past 500 episodes, weekly, positions itself as the largest show in the profession. Audience is accountants and bookkeepers who follow where the profession is going.

  • Takes guests and sponsors
  • NASBA-approved CPE through Earmark
  • Angle: the owner bottleneck as an operations problem, not a staffing one
Priority two

Unofficial QuickBooks Accountants Tight ICP

Alicia Katz Pollock and Hector Garcia. Smaller and more specific: firms running on QuickBooks, which is most of the ICP. Guests are usually product people, so an operations angle stands out.

  • Takes guests and sponsors
  • NASBA-approved CPE
  • Angle: what the tech stack does to the team rather than for it
Priority three

Oh My Fraud Brand play

Caleb Newquist. True crime for accountants, story-driven rather than advice-driven. Lower intent, but memorable, and it reaches the same listeners in a different mood.

  • Takes guests and sponsors
  • Angle: how weak internal process makes fraud possible, which is an operations story told sideways
Measurement

What we can and cannot know

Earmark publishes download numbers through the Open Podcast Prefix Project, and accountingpodcasts.com ranks shows by downloads. That gives us a real size estimate before committing time.

  • Ask each host for their per-episode average
  • Track a dedicated landing page per appearance
  • Judge on Ops Notes signups, not downloads
How we pitch. Hosts get dozens of vendor pitches a week. The pitch leads with a specific episode idea and the evidence behind it, never with a company introduction. One idea, one paragraph on why their listeners care, one line on who Chase is. The Gusto partnership is the credibility line that makes it land.
Sequencing. One show at a time, starting with Unofficial QuickBooks. It is the tightest ICP match and the easiest yes, which gives us a recorded appearance to reference when pitching The Accounting Podcast. Going straight to the biggest show without a recent appearance to point at is a weaker pitch.
